# Tymer voice set — Despina, London Underground announcer
#
# A public-address set: every line is delivered like a London Underground
# tannoy announcement — the style, not the vocabulary. Iconic announcement
# phrases are welcome ("mind the gap", "this is an important announcement",
# "end of the line"); literal transport nouns (station, train, platform,
# passengers, service) are not. The content is always time, work and breaks.
# Received Pronunciation, professional and impersonal — no endearments,
# no chumminess. Each announcement opens with a distinct attention-catcher
# before the information itself, and the opener escalates with the overtime
# ladder: "Your attention, please" → "service announcement" → "important
# announcement" → "urgent announcement" → "final call" → "emergency
# announcement". Same 33 events as the other sets; generates into the same
# event directories as interchangeable takes.
#
# @name below labels every file in this set `tube-N.wav`, so it never collides
# with the other sets' filenames.
#
# Regenerate: see sound-prompts/README.md

@voice Despina
@name tube
@profile Tymer — the station announcer
@scene
A London Underground platform. The announcer is unseen, speaking through the
station tannoy from a control room — the voice is projected to carry across a
long, tiled, slightly reverberant space, addressing everyone on the platform
rather than any one person. Trains pass; the announcements are the only human
voice in the room.
@style
Professional public-address delivery. Polished, impersonal courtesy — perfect
diction, even projection, every word articulated for a noisy platform. Formal
but never robotic; the warmth of a well-run service, not of a friend. No
endearments, no small talk.
@pace
Measured PA cadence — deliberate, evenly spaced. A [pause] tag separates the
opening attention line from the information: leave a tangible gap of silence
there, the way a station announcement lets the opener hang before continuing.
@accent
Received Pronunciation — crisp, polished London English, the classic Underground
announcement voice.
@context
The announcer marks the passage of a work or break period in the manner of a
station announcement: an attention-catching opening phrase first, a tangible
pause, then what is happening or what the listener must do. The delivery is
pure Underground tannoy; the words themselves are only ever about time, work
and breaks.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# overtime (work) — the escalation ladder. The opening phrase itself climbs:
# attention please → service announcement → important → urgent → final call →
# emergency. Courtesy thins out rung by rung; volume and pace take over.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

[overtime/006]
@style Courteous and light — a routine reminder, no edge yet.
@pace Unhurried, easy.
@text [courteous] Your attention, please. [pause] [gently] This period has now ended. Please begin bringing things to a close.

[overtime/012]
@style Matter-of-fact. The courtesy is procedural now, not warm.
@pace Steady, businesslike.
@text [clear] Your attention, please. [pause] [matter-of-fact] This period ended twelve minutes ago. Please find a stopping point now.

[overtime/018]
@style Firm. Politeness maintained, patience visibly thinning.
@pace Slightly clipped.
@text [firmly] This is a service announcement. [pause] [firm] You are running eighteen minutes over. Please bring this to a close.

[overtime/024]
@style Insistent. An instruction, and the listener should hear that.
@pace Brisk, pressing.
@text [insistent] This is an important announcement. [pause] [pointed] You are now twenty-four minutes over schedule. Close this down without further delay.

[overtime/030]
@style Stern. The measured authority of a delayed service losing patience.
@pace Deliberate, weighted, each word landing.
@text [sternly] This is an important announcement. [pause] [flat] Thirty minutes over schedule. This delay is now affecting your entire day. Stop work now.

[overtime/036]
@style Urgent and commanding. Nothing courteous remains.
@pace Fast, forceful, driving.
@text [urgently] This is an urgent announcement. [short pause] [commanding] Thirty-six minutes over schedule. Stop work immediately.

[overtime/042]
@style Sharp, absolute — the last time this will be said.
@pace Clipped, staccato, each phrase a strike.
@text [sharply] Final call. This is the final call. [short pause] [commanding] You are forty-two minutes past time. Put it down and move on. Now.

[overtime/048]
@style
Maximum urgency — the top of the ladder. Full emergency-broadcast pressure:
loud, driving, relentless, the intensity keeps climbing and never releases.
Not cold — pressing.
@pace Driving and emphatic. Weight on every word, pushing forward, never dragging.
@text [urgently] This is an emergency announcement. [short pause] [insistent] Your time expired forty-eight minutes ago. This stops immediately. Do not delay any further.
